Where most salon sites quietly lose business

These are the problems we see on almost every salon website we audit — and exactly what we fix.

Booking friction costs your busiest hours

Clients who can’t instantly see slots and book them will happily take them to a competitor’s one-tap system. Speed to booking is the entire conversion.

No prices on the menu

Hair and spa services are researched budgets. A menu without pricing makes you look expensive by default and sends price-sensitive clients away without a call.

The vibe is unreadable in a template

A generic template reads “corporate” or “startup”, not “curly-girl specialist” or “quiet luxury spa”. Style is a booking signal and most themes can’t carry it.

What we’d build for you

salon website questions, answered

Should online booking replace phone booking?

Neither. You get a phone-first layout AND online booking — some clients will always call, and the ones who won’t are the ones the button captures.

Do you integrate with my current booking software?

If you use a scheduler (Fresha, Vagaro, etc.), we design the site around it. If not, we scope a simple booking model into the build.

Can you make it feel premium without stockiness?

Yes — typography, spacing, brand color and image treatment carry the vibe, and they’re exactly the template parts we customize first.

Do I own the design?

Completely. Code in your GitHub from day one, IP transferred in writing, and you can take it to any developer later.

Where most landscaping sites quietly lose business

These are the problems we see on almost every landscaping website we audit — and exactly what we fix.

Portfolio photos that take too long to load

Homeowners judge landscapers on the work. A slow gallery or slideshow on a dated template makes them bounce straight to the next company instead of requesting an estimate.

One business, three services, zero structure

Design/build, lawn maintenance and irrigation are bought differently and at different seasons. Squeezing them onto one page means each one loses.

Nobody knows your true service area

Landscapers live and die by their radius. Vague wording here costs both search visibility and filtering — homeowners skip crews they think won’t come.

Clear coverage areas with maps and nearby-city pages so search engines and homeowners both understand your radius.

landscaping website questions, answered

How do you make a portfolio site feel fast on mobile?

Real image optimization — compressed next-gen formats, responsive sizes and lazy loading — not a plug-in that says “fast.” Galleries are the heaviest part of a landscaper site and the easiest to get right.

My business is mostly recurring lawn care — do I still need a portfolio?

Even a maintenance business wins on proof. A lean site with a small gallery, pricing transparency and an easy signup reduces the friction a homeowner feels before calling you.

Do you handle estimates, contracts and scheduling?

Online estimate requests and landing pages per service are in scope. Full scheduling/contracting software is an upsell we can scope separately on the maintenance retainer.

Can I edit content myself after launch?

Yes. You get an owner’s manual in plain English, and optional CMS/blog training. You own the code, so there is no hostage situation.

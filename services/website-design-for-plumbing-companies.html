Where most plumbing sites quietly lose business

These are the problems we see on almost every plumbing website we audit — and exactly what we fix.

The call button is impossible to find

A buried contact page costs emergency jobs. A homeowner with a burst pipe compares you for the ten seconds it takes to tap a number — and yours must be there first.

Trust is everything and most sites don’t show it

License numbers, insured details, review counts and guarantee copy win the “will they show up and not overcharge” moment. Most plumber sites bury this below stock photos.

One price, many services

Repair, drain cleaning, water heaters, repiping — each is a separate buying decision with its own keyword. Flat pages force customers to guess whether you do their job.

plumbing website questions, answered

How fast does an emergency customer find my number?

With a sticky call bar and header phone on a phone-first layout, the number is reachable in under a second and on every page. That is the standard we build to.

Should I publish prices on the site?

Where you set flat rates, yes — price anchoring converts. Where jobs vary, a “what repairs typically cost” guide is proven to earn more qualified calls than silence.

Can you handle scheduling and after-hours booking?

Online booking and after-hours request forms are in scope. If you want dispatch software, we can scope it as a separate automation build.

What happens if I want changes after launch?

30 days of warranty fixes are included, and you own the code plus an editing manual. Optional maintenance retainers cover anything after that.

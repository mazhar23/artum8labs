Decision Guide

Custom Patient Portal vs Your EHR's Built-in Portal

The EHR portal is the safe default. A custom portal is justified when patient experience, service-line workflows, or multi-system access become a competitive requirement rather than an afterthought.

Short answer

Extend the EHR portal when it covers most patient tasks and you run a single EHR. Build custom when service lines need workflows, branding, or multi-EHR access the vendor portal cannot support.

Side-by-side comparison

Dimension EHR Built-in Portal Custom Patient Portal
Upfront costLower; often bundled with the EHRHigher; design, build, and integrations
ControlLimited to vendor features and brandingFull control over UX and workflows
WorkflowsStandard patient tasksTailored intake, triage, billing, and follow-up
IntegrationNative to one EHRFHIR, HL7, and API connections across systems
Multi-EHR supportUsually noYes, with a unified patient record
Speed to launchFaster; configuration only6–12 weeks for a scoped build
Best forSingle-EHR practices with standard needsHealthtech products, multi-site groups, specialty workflows

Ranges are directional. Exact scope, price, and dates are fixed in a written proposal after a short discovery call.

Which should you choose?

Choose EHR Built-in Portal if…

  • You run one EHR and its portal covers scheduling, records, and messaging.
  • Your team does not have engineering capacity to maintain a portal.
  • Patient experience is not a primary differentiator for your growth.

Choose Custom Patient Portal if…

  • You operate multiple EHRs or sites and need one patient experience.
  • Specialty workflows do not fit the vendor portal.
  • Patient acquisition and retention depend on a branded digital front door.

How Artum8 Labs approaches it

Frequently asked questions

Can a custom portal integrate with Epic or Cerner?

Yes, through FHIR R4 and HL7 v2 interfaces where the health system exposes them. Integration scope is confirmed during discovery.

Is a custom portal HIPAA-ready?

We design for HIPAA from the start: access control, audit logging, encryption, and business associate agreements. Final compliance review remains with your organization.

Should we build or extend?

Extend if one EHR portal covers your core tasks. Build if multi-EHR access, specialty workflows, or patient experience are strategic requirements.

How long does a custom portal take?

A scoped build typically takes 6–12 weeks, depending on integrations, data migration, and clinical review cycles.

What about accessibility?

We build toward WCAG 2.2 AA, including keyboard navigation, contrast, and screen-reader-friendly forms, because patient portals serve a wide range of users.

Tell us the constraint you are solving for. We will recommend the simpler option first, even when it means a smaller engagement.
